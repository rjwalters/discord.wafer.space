As mentioned previously, it might be helpful to share a public reference quote for a representative deviation, broken down just enough to let people feel a bit how much of a cost impact the various aspects of those changes are. 

For example, a quote for "standard but the PCB layout is different but it still fits the same fixture and it has the same bond geometry" and/or "quarter standard but we skip bonding of one/two/three sides with no other changes to the bond geometry".

Not because necessarily those are exactly what the people want, but because they show how affordable customization with only very minor NRE costs over top of the default-COB-offer baseline are.

I'm assuming you're handling the manufacturing of the default-COB-offer for wafer.space and mithro is willing to let you do that under contract terms where you'd be allowed to recycle "the shared NRE work from producing that default-COB-offer" for customized COB offerings sold to wafer.space customers.

Perhaps even offer a reasonable fee structure as a "list price" (subject to negotiation) that partially encodes the labor-saving measures of the bulk edits of linear pad arrays and/or similar aspects so a customer could calculate themselves an expected rough list price for e.g. a 3 sided die with one of those sides densified to make up for the lost signal pads (vs. the default-COB-offer for that very slot), considering that such a change would open up a good chunk of extra core area that could be critical shortly before GDS deadline if, say, a design flaw came up and the sudden fix for it doesn't allow for enough interactive tuning of RTL and PnR.

Being able to quickly calculate an expected cost for the "custom bonding to unlock extra core area to lessen PnR density pressure, making timing closure on the adjusted design much easier" escape-hatch from a risky tapeout, would IMO be very useful.

[2026-11-16 12:22 a.m.] namibj
The caps are populated; it's iirc 0.8mil al wire; trace current limits will depend on thermal surroundings.

If you need/want much more than the reasonable power from the bond wires because you're doing a low-loss switched-mode power converter, you _might_ consider helping working towards qualifying a flip-chip option rated for considerably higher current than the thin wire bonds; there's the `FC01` test dies from Run2; particularly if you have any affordable way (especially there should be a path to scaling it to one-or-more slots of Run4 undergoing the process with their full quantity, post-dicing (||only exception w.r.t. pre/post dicing would be if it's cheap enough and won't conflict with the COB processing for the other slots, in which case doing it pre-dicing might possibly be discussed||)) to plate the existing aluminium pads up in thickness to form bumps which could then via their raised structure define flip-chip bond areas.
